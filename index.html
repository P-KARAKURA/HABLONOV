ACTIVITY 2 · READING

The Story: The Tiger and the Donkey

One day, a donkey and a tiger had a disagreement about the colour of the grass. The donkey insisted that the grass was blue, while the tiger argued that it was green.

The argument continued, but neither animal wanted to change its opinion. The tiger believed that the truth always prevailed, so he proposed that they take the matter to the lion, the King of the Jungle.

When they arrived, the lion was sitting on his throne. The donkey approached him and asked him to settle their disagreement. The lion listened to the donkey and agreed with him.

The donkey became very happy. However, the tiger was surprised. He asked the lion why he had agreed with the donkey when the grass was clearly green.

The lion sentenced the tiger to five years of silence. The tiger was confused and asked why he had been punished for telling the truth.

The lion explained that the punishment was not for telling the truth. It was because the tiger had wasted his time arguing with someone who refused to listen.

The lion reminded him that wise creatures never waste time arguing with people who refuse to listen, learn or understand.

Moral: Do not waste your time arguing with people who refuse to listen, learn or understand.

ACTIVITY 3 · GRAMMAR

Three Essential English Tenses

A. Simple Present Tense

We use the Simple Present to describe habits, routines, general truths, facts and situations that are generally stable.

Examples:

  • The sun rises in the east.
  • She teaches English every evening.
  • Wise people do not waste time.
  • He understands the importance of education.

Form: Subject + base form of the verb. Add -s or -es with he, she and it.

B. Present Continuous Tense

We use the Present Continuous for actions happening now, temporary situations, changing situations and arranged future events.

Examples:

  • The students are discussing the story now.
  • She is staying with her aunt this week.
  • More people are learning digital skills.
  • We are meeting the coordinator tomorrow.

Form: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing.

C. Simple Past Tense

We use the Simple Past for completed actions and events that happened at a definite or understood time in the past.

Examples:

  • The tiger challenged the donkey.
  • The lion listened to their disagreement.
  • They visited the village yesterday.
  • She completed her assignment last night.

Form: Subject + past form of the verb.

D. Understanding the Differences

Consider how the tense changes the meaning of these sentences:

  • Regular activity: He works at the school.
  • Temporary activity: He is working from home this week.
  • Completed past activity: He worked there last year.

E. Stative Verbs

Stative verbs describe states, feelings, possession, thoughts, opinions and senses rather than physical actions. They are not normally used in continuous forms.

Common examples include: know, believe, understand, own, belong, prefer, need and seem.

Usually correct: I understand the lesson.

Usually incorrect: I am understanding the lesson.

However, some verbs can be stative or dynamic depending on their meaning.

Opinion: I think you are right.

Mental activity: I am thinking about the problem.

Feeling: I feel sad.

Temporary physical experience: I am feeling the fabric.

F. Learning Reminder

Do not choose a tense simply because of a time expression. First identify the meaning of the sentence: is it a habit, an action in progress, a temporary situation or a completed past event?
