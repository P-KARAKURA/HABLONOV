ACTIVITY 1 · GRAMMAR

What are FANBOYS?

FANBOYS is a memory aid for seven coordinating conjunctions. They connect words, phrases or independent clauses of equal grammatical importance.

F — For

Shows a reason or explanation.

I stayed inside, for it was raining.

A — And

Adds information.

She reads and writes every day.

N — Nor

Adds a negative idea.

He did not call, nor did he send a message.

B — But

Shows contrast.

The task was difficult, but we completed it.

O — Or

Shows a choice or alternative.

You can revise now, or you can revise later.

Y — Yet

Shows an unexpected contrast.

She was tired, yet she continued working.

S — So

Shows a result or consequence.

It was raining, so we stayed indoors.

Important: Independent clauses

An independent clause has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought.

The students were tired. They continued studying.

Joined with a coordinating conjunction:

The students were tired, yet they continued studying.

Punctuation rule

When a coordinating conjunction joins two independent clauses, place a comma before the conjunction.

I wanted to attend the meeting, but I was ill.

When joining only two words or phrases, a comma is generally not needed.

She bought notebooks and pens.

Pay special attention to YET

"Yet" can be a coordinating conjunction showing contrast. It can also be an adverb meaning "until now" or "so far."

  • Conjunction: He was inexperienced, yet he succeeded.
  • Adverb: He has not arrived yet.
  • Adverb in a question: Have you finished yet?

Do not confuse these grammatical roles. In the first example, "yet" joins contrasting independent clauses. In the other examples, it modifies the verb phrase.

Do not confuse FOR

As a FANBOYS conjunction, "for" introduces a reason:

She went home, for she was exhausted.

As a preposition, "for" can express duration:

She studied for three hours.

These are different grammatical uses. Stage 3 explores time expressions in more detail.

ACTIVITY 2 · PRACTICE

Build stronger sentences

Compare the meaning of each sentence:

1. He was tired, but he stopped working.

2. He was tired, yet he continued working.

3. He was tired, so he went to bed.

The conjunction changes the relationship between the ideas. Choose it according to the meaning you intend to communicate.
